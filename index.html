Read this before you knock

Florida requires a permit to knock cold doors. A “home solicitation sale” — selling goods or services at somebody’s house without being invited — needs a Home Solicitation Sale permit from the Clerk of Court in that county under F.S. 501.022. Brevard charges $100 plus a $24 background check, takes fingerprints, and the permit runs one year. Contractors are not on the exemption list. Invited visits are.

  • You can’t sign a roof. F.S. 489.113(3) makes a Certified Building Contractor subcontract roofing, and Covenant has no approved roofer yet. Knock, look, list — don’t sign the roof scope.
  • Never say “you have damage” before you’ve been on the roof, and never say what insurance will cover. The carrier decides.
  • F.S. 489.147 — no paying or waiving deductibles, no gifts for signing, no referral money, and no interpreting the policy or adjusting the claim without a public adjuster licence.
  • Hours: knock from about 3:30pm to dusk. Before 4 is empty, after dark is rude.

Sources: F.S. 501.022 · Brevard Clerk · F.S. 489.113(3) · F.S. 489.147

What the numbers here mean

  • Hail size is what a person on the ground reported to the National Weather Service that day. One inch is where shingles start taking real damage.
  • Days to file counts down one year from the date of the storm — F.S. 627.70132. A supplement runs eighteen months.
  • Year built comes from the Florida Department of Revenue’s 2025 tax roll. It is the house’s age, not the roof’s. A 1989 house can wear a 2019 roof. The record points you at a street; the roof tells the truth.
